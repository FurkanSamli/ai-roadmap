## g3 | rl | Reinforcement Learning | Reinforcement Learning
> Agents that learn by trial, error and reward.
? Like teaching a dog tricks: reward the right behavior, not the wrong one. The agent tries thousands of times and finds the strategy that earns the most reward.
- [1] Core concepts
  ? The basic vocabulary of RL.
  - [1] Agent, environment, state, action, reward
    ? A player (agent) in a game (environment) looks at the screen (state), presses a button (action), gets points (reward).
  - [1] Exploration vs exploitation
    ? Go to the good restaurant you know, or try a new one that might be better?
  - [2] Markov Decision Process (MDP)
    ? The mathematical frame for RL problems: states, actions, transition probabilities and rewards.
  - [2] Policy & value functions
    > V(s), Q(s, a)
    ? A policy answers “what do I do here?”, a value function answers “how good is this situation?”.
  - [2] Bellman equations
    ? A state's value = the reward now + the value of the next state; most RL algorithms rest on this simple idea.
  - [2] Discount factor & return
    ? A reward now is worth more than one tomorrow; this setting decides how far ahead the agent thinks.
- [2] Multi-armed bandits
  > ε-greedy, UCB, Thompson sampling
  ? Finding which of many slot machines pays best while losing as little as possible; used in ads and A/B testing.
- [2] Dynamic programming
  > Value iteration, policy iteration
  ? Computing the best strategy directly when you fully know the environment's rules.
- [2] Model-free methods
  ? Learning without knowing the environment's rules, just by trying.
  - [2] Monte Carlo methods
    ? Playing a whole episode, then judging all the moves by how it ended.
  - [2] Temporal difference learning (TD)
    ? Updating your estimate at every step based on the next estimate, without waiting for the end.
  - [2] Q-learning & SARSA
    ? Filling in a table of how good each action is in each state by trying things out.
- [3] Deep reinforcement learning
  ? Combining RL with neural networks to solve complex problems from Atari to robots.
  - [3] Deep Q-Network (DQN)
    ? Using a neural network instead of a Q-table; the first big success at learning Atari games from the screen.
  - [3] Policy gradient & REINFORCE
    ? Making good behaviors directly more likely, without keeping a value table.
  - [3] Actor-Critic
    > A2C, A3C
    ? One network decides (actor), another judges how good that decision was (critic).
  - [3] PPO
    > One of the most widely used; also used in RLHF
    ? A stable, popular method that updates the policy without changing it “too much” at each step.
  - [3] SAC, DDPG, TD3
    > For continuous action spaces
    ? Algorithms for agents choosing continuous values, like a steering angle or robot arm torque.
- [3] Model-based RL
  > AlphaZero, MuZero, world models
  ? The agent learns how the environment works and plans by “simulating” it in its head.
- [3] Imitation learning & inverse RL
  ? Learning by watching an expert instead of defining rewards, like learning to drive from a good driver's recordings.
- [3] Multi-agent RL
  ? Several learning agents that compete or cooperate.
- [3] RL from human feedback (RLHF)
  > Used to align LLMs
  ? The reward comes from people's preferences, not a formula; that's where models like ChatGPT get “polite and helpful”.
- [2] Tools
  > Gymnasium, Stable-Baselines3, PettingZoo
  ? Ready-made game and simulation environments, plus ready-made RL algorithms.
- [1] Applications
  > Games, robotics, self-driving, recommenders, resource optimization
  ? Anywhere you need a sequence of decisions, from chess to cooling data centers.
