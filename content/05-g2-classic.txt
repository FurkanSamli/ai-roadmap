## g2 | classic | Classical (Symbolic) AI | Classical AI
> AI before learning: search, logic, planning. Usually the first half of AI textbooks.
? Before machine learning took off, AI meant searching cleverly and reasoning with logic. These ideas still live in navigation, games and planning.
- [1] Intelligent agents
  > Systems that perceive and act; types of environments
  ? Anything that senses its surroundings, decides and acts is an agent: a robot vacuum or a chess program.
- [1] Search algorithms
  ? Finding a path from start to goal by systematically trying possible steps, like a maps app finding a route.
  - [1] Uninformed search
    > BFS, DFS, uniform cost search
    ? Searching with no hint about where the goal is: check nearby first (BFS) or follow one path to the end (DFS).
  - [1] Heuristic search & A*
    > Greedy best-first, A*
    ? Prioritizing the right direction with a guess like “how far is the goal as the crow flies?”; common in games and maps.
  - [2] Local search
    > Hill climbing, simulated annealing
    ? Start from a solution and look for better ones with small changes; sometimes a bad step saves you from getting stuck.
  - [2] Game trees: minimax & alpha-beta
    ? Choosing moves assuming the opponent also plays their best; the classic method of chess and checkers programs.
  - [3] Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)
    > One of AlphaGo's ingredients
    ? Trying each move with thousands of random playouts and picking the one that wins most.
- [2] Constraint satisfaction problems (CSP)
  > Sudoku, timetabling, scheduling
  ? Finding a solution that obeys all rules at once, like a timetable where no lecturer has two classes at the same time.
- [2] Knowledge representation & reasoning
  ? Writing knowledge in a form a computer can reason with, and deriving new conclusions from it.
  - [1] Expert systems & rule-based systems
    ? Turning an expert's knowledge into hundreds of “if... then...” rules; widely used in medicine and industry in the 80s.
  - [2] Propositional & first-order logic
    ? Turning sentences into formulas with true/false values: “All humans are mortal, Socrates is human, so...”.
  - [2] Inference methods
    > Forward and backward chaining, resolution
    ? Automatic ways of deriving new facts from known facts using rules.
  - [2] Ontologies & knowledge graphs
    ? Storing concepts and their relations as a network: “Ingolstadt is in Bavaria, Bavaria is in Germany”. Search engines use them.
- [3] Automated planning
  > STRIPS, PDDL
  ? Automatically working out which actions to take, in which order, to reach a goal; for robots and logistics.
- [2] Probabilistic reasoning
  ? When the world is uncertain, logic isn't enough; you reason with probabilities.
  - [2] Bayesian networks
    ? Showing how events influence each other with an arrow diagram and computing probabilities: rain, sprinkler, wet grass.
  - [2] Hidden Markov Models (HMM)
    ? Guessing a hidden state from observed clues; speech recognition used to be done this way.
  - [2] Markov Decision Processes (MDP)
    > The mathematical framework of reinforcement learning
    ? The standard way to describe decision problems with states, actions and rewards.
- [2] Evolutionary & nature-inspired algorithms
  ? Searching for solutions by imitating natural selection or ant colonies.
  - [2] Genetic algorithms
    ? Create a “population” of solutions, breed the good ones, add some mutation, repeat for generations.
  - [3] Particle swarm & ant colony
    ? Optimization methods inspired by bird flocks and ants finding food.
- [2] Fuzzy logic
  ? Reasoning not just with true/false but with in-between values like “a bit warm”; used in washing machines and controllers.
- [3] Multi-agent systems & game theory
  ? Situations where several agents cooperate or compete, like auctions or traffic.
