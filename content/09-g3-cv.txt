## g3 | cv | Computer Vision | Computer Vision
> Machines understanding images and video.
? Turning camera pixels into meaning like “that's a pedestrian, that's a traffic light”. Self-driving cars, medical imaging and quality control are built on it.
- [1] Image processing basics
  ? How images are stored in a computer and processed with classic methods.
  - [1] Pixels, channels, color spaces
    > RGB, HSV, grayscale
    ? An image is a table of red, green and blue numbers.
  - [1] Filters & convolution
    ? Sliding a small window over an image to blur or sharpen it; the classic ancestor of CNNs.
  - [1] Libraries
    > OpenCV, Pillow
    ? Standard tools for reading, cropping, rotating images and classic vision operations.
  - [2] Edge & corner detection
    > Sobel, Canny, Harris
    ? Finding where brightness changes suddenly, which is usually where object borders are.
  - [2] Thresholding & morphology
    ? Turning an image black-and-white and cleaning up small specks; used in document scanning and industrial inspection.
  - [3] Classic features
    > SIFT, HOG
    ? Hand-designed pattern descriptors used to describe images before deep learning.
- [1] Image classification
  > “What's in this image?”
  ? Giving the whole image one label: cat, dog, car.
- [2] Object detection
  > “What is where?”
  ? Drawing a box around every object in an image and naming it; a core task in self-driving.
  - [2] One-stage detectors
    > The YOLO family, SSD
    ? Fast models that find all objects in a single look; for real-time applications.
  - [3] Two-stage detectors
    > R-CNN, Fast R-CNN, Faster R-CNN
    ? Slower but precise models that first propose “something might be here” regions, then classify each.
  - [3] Transformer-based detection
    > DETR
    ? Doing object detection directly as set prediction with a transformer.
- [2] Segmentation
  > Pixel-level understanding
  ? Instead of boxes, deciding what every pixel belongs to: road, sidewalk, pedestrian, sky.
  - [2] Semantic, instance & panoptic segmentation
    ? Semantic: “these are car pixels”. Instance: “this is car 1, that is car 2”. Panoptic: both at once.
  - [2] U-Net & Mask R-CNN
    ? Classic segmentation architectures; U-Net is especially common in medical imaging.
  - [3] Segment Anything (SAM)
    ? A general model that can cut out any object from an image with a single click.
- [2] Vision Transformer (ViT)
  ? Cutting an image into small squares and feeding each to a transformer like a word; competes with CNNs on big data.
- [2] Face detection & recognition
  ? Finding faces and recognizing whose they are, from phone unlocking to security systems; privacy debates start here too.
- [2] OCR & document understanding
  ? Reading text in images and pulling information out of documents like invoices and forms.
- [3] Pose estimation & tracking
  ? Locating a person's skeleton joints and following objects through a video.
- [3] Video understanding
  > Action recognition, temporal modeling
  ? Understanding not just frames but what happens over time: “someone fell”, “the car changed lanes”.
- [3] 3D vision
  ? Understanding the 3-D world from 2-D images and sensors.
  - [3] Depth estimation & stereo vision
    ? Estimating how far away things are from one or two cameras.
  - [3] Point clouds
    ? Working with millions of 3-D points from sensors like LiDAR; very important for self-driving cars.
  - [3] NeRF & Gaussian Splatting
    ? Building a realistic 3-D scene you can walk around from a handful of photos.
- [3] Vision-language models (VLM)
  > CLIP; multimodal LLMs that understand images
  ? Models that can look at an image and talk about it: “what do you see in this chart?”
- [2] Libraries
  > torchvision, Ultralytics, Detectron2, timm
  ? Ready-made vision models and training tools; with Ultralytics you can train YOLO in a few lines.
