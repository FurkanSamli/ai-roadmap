## g1 | basics | Core Concepts & History | Concepts & History
> What AI is, where it came from; the big picture of the field.
? The entry gate of the map: what the word “AI” means and how we got here.
- [1] What is AI?
  > Systems that do tasks needing human intelligence: perceiving, reasoning, learning, deciding
  ? If a computer does something that normally needs a human brain, like recognizing faces, playing chess or writing text, we call it AI.
- [1] Types of AI
  ? Classifying AI by how “general” it is.
  - [1] Narrow AI (ANI)
    > Systems good at one task; almost all AI in use today
    ? AI that is good at one thing. A chess engine plays brilliant chess but can't give you a recipe.
  - [1] Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)
    > A system that learns and acts in any domain like a human; its definition and timing are still debated
    ? An AI that can learn and think about anything, like a person. When it will arrive, and even what exactly it means, is debated.
  - [2] Superintelligence (ASI)
    > Hypothetical intelligence surpassing humans in every domain
    ? A hypothetical AI smarter than the best humans at everything. For now, a thought experiment.
- [1] Approaches
  ? There are two big ways to build AI: write the rules by hand, or let the machine learn them from data.
  - [1] Symbolic AI
    > Knowledge hand-coded as rules and logic
    ? AI where people write rules like “if fever and cough, maybe flu”.
  - [1] Connectionism
    > Neural networks; knowledge learned from data
    ? AI inspired by brain neurons that works out the rules from examples. Today's deep learning comes from here.
  - [3] Neuro-symbolic AI
    > Efforts to combine both approaches
    ? Research that tries to combine the learning power of neural nets with the clarity of logical rules.
- [1] History
  ? AI's roughly 75-year story: big hype, disappointments and comebacks.
  - 1950 · The Turing Test
    > Alan Turing's question: “Can machines think?”
    ? If you chat with a machine and can't tell whether it's human, should it count as intelligent? Turing proposed this test.
  - 1956 · Dartmouth Workshop
    > Birth of the term “Artificial Intelligence”
    ? A group of scientists met for a summer and gave the field its name, “artificial intelligence”.
  - 1958 · The Perceptron
    > The first learning artificial neuron (Rosenblatt)
    ? The first simple artificial neuron that could learn from examples; the great-grandparent of today's giant networks.
  - AI winters
    > Loss of funding and interest in the 1970s and late 1980s after unmet expectations
    ? AI promised a lot; when results didn't come, money and interest dried up. The field hibernated twice.
  - 1986 · Backpropagation
    > Backpropagation popularized (Rumelhart, Hinton, Williams); expert systems in the same era
    ? A practical way to train multi-layer neural networks spread. All neural networks are still trained this way.
  - 1997 · Deep Blue
    > Beat world chess champion Kasparov
    ? IBM's computer beat the world chess champion. It didn't learn; it searched incredibly fast.
  - 2012 · AlexNet
    > A GPU-trained CNN that started the deep learning revolution
    ? A neural net trained on graphics cards won an image recognition contest by a mile, and everyone turned to deep learning.
  - 2016 · AlphaGo
    > Beat Lee Sedol at Go (deep learning + reinforcement learning + MCTS)
    ? It beat one of the world's best at Go, far more complex than chess, learning partly by playing against itself.
  - 2017 · The Transformer
    > The “Attention Is All You Need” paper; the basis of today's LLMs
    ? A new architecture where every word can “look at” every other word. ChatGPT and similar models are built on it.
  - 2022+ · ChatGPT and the LLM boom
    > Generative AI goes mainstream
    ? Chatting AIs became available to everyone and AI entered everyday life.
- [2] Philosophy & cognitive science
  ? Big questions like “what is intelligence, can a machine really understand?”
  - [2] The Chinese Room
    > Whether manipulating symbols amounts to “understanding”
    ? If someone who knows no Chinese answers Chinese questions correctly with a rulebook, do they “understand” Chinese? The same question is asked of machines.
  - [3] Debates on consciousness
    ? Can an AI have experiences, feelings or consciousness? An open philosophical question without a settled answer.
  - [2] Cognitive science & neuroscience
    > The biological inspiration behind neural nets
    ? How the brain works gives AI ideas, and AI models in turn help us study the brain.
