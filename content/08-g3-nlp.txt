## g3 | nlp | Natural Language Processing (NLP) & Large Language Models (LLM) | NLP & LLMs
> Understanding and generating text; currently the busiest part of the field.
? Computers understanding and writing human language. ChatGPT, translation, summarization and chatbots all live in this cluster.
- [1] Classical NLP
  ? Ways of working with text before deep learning; still useful for fast, simple solutions.
  - [1] Text preprocessing
    > Tokenization, stop words, stemming, lemmatization
    ? Splitting text into pieces, dropping filler words like “and, a”, reducing “running” to its root “run”.
  - [1] Bag of Words & TF-IDF
    ? Turning text into a list of word counts; TF-IDF gives more weight to rare but meaningful words.
  - [1] Sentiment analysis
    ? Working out whether a review is positive or negative.
  - [1] Libraries
    > NLTK, spaCy; Zemberek for Turkish
    ? Ready-made text tools; agglutinative languages like Turkish have their own challenges.
  - [2] N-gram language models
    ? Predicting the next word from the last few words; a very simple ancestor of LLMs.
  - [2] Part-of-speech tagging (POS)
    ? Finding whether each word is a noun, verb, adjective...
  - [2] Named entity recognition (NER)
    ? Finding and tagging names of people, places, companies and dates: “Audi” is a company, “Ingolstadt” a city.
  - [3] Syntactic parsing
    ? Building a sentence's grammar tree: what's the subject, the verb, which word depends on which.
- [2] Word embeddings
  > Word2Vec, GloVe, FastText
  ? Turning each word into a vector so words with similar meanings sit close together.
- [2] Tokenizers
  > BPE, WordPiece, SentencePiece
  ? LLMs see “tokens”, not words. “Ingolstadt” might be split into several tokens, and pricing is by token count too.
- [2] Transformer-based language models
  ? The three main families of today's language models.
  - [2] Encoder models
    > BERT, RoBERTa, BERTurk for Turkish
    ? Masters of understanding text: classification, search, entity recognition. They don't generate text.
  - [2] Decoder models
    > The GPT family, Llama, Claude, Gemini
    ? Models that generate text word by word; every chatbot belongs to this family.
  - [2] Encoder-decoder models
    > T5, BART
    ? Models that first read and understand, then write; a natural fit for translation and summarization.
- [3] LLM training lifecycle
  ? The stages that turn raw text into a helpful assistant.
  - [2] Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)
    > LoRA, QLoRA, adapters
    ? Adapting a huge model by training only small add-on pieces instead of the whole thing; possible even on a single GPU.
  - [3] Pretraining
    > Next-token prediction on enormous text
    ? Reading a large part of the internet and playing “what's the next word?” trillions of times; language and world knowledge come from here.
  - [3] Instruction tuning (SFT)
    ? Teaching the pretrained model to “behave like an assistant” with question-answer examples.
  - [3] Alignment
    > RLHF, DPO, Constitutional AI
    ? Steering the model by which answers people prefer, so it's more helpful, honest and harmless.
  - [3] Reasoning models
    > Chain-of-thought training, test-time compute
    ? Models that “think” step by step before answering; much better at hard math and coding questions.
- [1] Prompt engineering
  ? The craft of telling an LLM clearly what you want; the same model does far better with a good prompt.
  - [1] Zero-shot & few-shot
    ? Asking with no examples, or showing a few examples to say “do it like this”.
  - [1] System prompts, roles & format
    ? Telling the model up front who it is, how to behave and what format to answer in.
  - [1] Chain-of-thought
    ? Saying “think step by step”; it noticeably improves accuracy on hard questions.
  - [2] Structured output
    > JSON schema
    ? Asking the model for tidy data a program can read (e.g. JSON) instead of free text.
- [2] Inference
  ? How a trained LLM produces answers, and how to make that fast.
  - [1] Context window
    ? How much text the model can “see” at once; when it fills up, early information falls out.
  - [2] Decoding
    > Greedy, beam search, top-k, top-p, temperature
    ? At each step the model gives probabilities to possible words; these settings decide which gets picked. Higher temperature means more creative, more random answers.
  - [3] KV cache
    ? Storing the computations for earlier words so they aren't redone; speeds up generation a lot but eats memory.
  - [3] Speculative decoding
    ? A small fast model guesses a few words and the big model checks them in one go; faster without losing quality.
- [2] RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)
  > Connecting the model to an outside knowledge source
  ? Finding relevant documents and putting them in front of the model before it answers, so it can use things it never learned, like company docs or lecture notes.
  - [2] Chunking
    ? Splitting long documents into small pieces the model can handle; chunk size affects results a lot.
  - [2] Embedding models
    ? Models that turn text chunks into meaning vectors; the heart of retrieval.
  - [2] Vector databases
    > FAISS, Chroma, Qdrant, Weaviate, Pinecone, pgvector
    ? Stores that find the most similar vectors among millions in milliseconds.
  - [3] Reranking & hybrid search
    > BM25 + vector search
    ? Combining keyword matching with meaning-based search, then reordering results with a smarter model.
  - [3] GraphRAG
    ? Building a knowledge graph from documents and searching over it; strong at connecting scattered facts.
- [2] LLM agents
  ? LLM systems that don't just answer but use tools and do work step by step.
  - [2] Tool use / function calling
    ? The model being able to call a calculator, a search engine or your own code.
  - [2] ReAct & planning
    ? The loop “think, act, look at the result, think again”; how multi-step tasks get solved.
  - [2] Model Context Protocol (MCP)
    > A standard for connecting models to tools and data sources
    ? A common “plug” standard for connecting AI models to tools like calendars, files or databases.
  - [3] Memory management
    ? Deciding what an agent keeps and what it summarizes or forgets during long tasks.
  - [3] Multi-agent systems
    ? Several LLMs with different roles working together: one plans, one writes, one checks.
- [2] Evaluation
  ? Measuring whether a language model is actually good; surprisingly hard.
  - [2] Classic metrics
    > BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore
    ? Scoring generated text by word or meaning overlap with a reference text.
  - [2] Benchmarks
    > MMLU, HumanEval, GSM8K, SWE-bench
    ? Standard exams models take: general knowledge, coding, math, real software bugs.
  - [2] LLM-as-a-judge
    ? One model grading another model's answers; fast but with its own biases.
  - [3] Hallucination detection
    ? Catching information the model confidently makes up.
- [1] Application tasks
  > Translation, summarization, question answering, chatbots, code generation, information extraction
  ? NLP in daily life: translating with DeepL, summarizing a long PDF, coding assistants.
- [1] Tools
  > Hugging Face, LangChain, LlamaIndex, Ollama, llama.cpp, vLLM
  ? Tools for building LLM apps and running models on your own computer.
